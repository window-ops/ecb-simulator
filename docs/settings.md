# Settings

[Back to the README](../README.md) · [Developing](developing.md)

Settings opens from the toolbar, and the start page has Quick settings for the most used ones. The settings are kept in this browser apart from the saves, so the game data can be deleted without them; [Saved state](saved-state.md) lists every setting and its default.

Each page shows its settings in cards, one under the other, as KDE's settings pages group related settings. A card's title stands above it, as KDE's form headers do, styled as the headings of the groups of saves: at the left edge, 10px above and below it, none above the first. Each title and its card make one section. Each card reads as a table: a subtle dotted line parts each setting from the next, a note staying close to what it explains: a note under a setting has no space above it, a note that opens a card has no space and no line below it, and a note at the foot of a card has a dotted line and its space above it. Settings > Appearance > Dialogs > Guide lines turns these lines off (`guideLines`). The card has no padding above or below its rows: each setting is a 36px row with 8px above and below it (`--gap-xs-readable`, the one spacing off the multiples of five), which keeps the settings apart with or without the lines, its label in the middle of its control; on a phone the label stands over its control with 10px between them. The list of categories names the page, so the page's heading is for screen readers only, as is the line saying that the debug tools are disabled while debug mode is off. A page drawn anew keeps its scroll and focus before the browser paints, so it does not flicker.

- **Appearance**: Colors has Breeze Light, Breeze Dark or the system's choice, Plasma's ten accent colors or any other, and the logo in the text color or the accent color. Positions has the corner the notifications open in (on a phone, where a notification takes nearly the whole width, only top or bottom), whether the indicators scroll with the page, where the status shows, and Not focused, the pill in the title bar while the game does not have the focus (on by default). Sizes has a readable page width and a window width for large screens, each with a switch and a slider, accountant mode and rows of cards. Dialogs has their height and the guide lines of the settings cards. Title screen has a title screen that is plain, one of eight themes or a custom one (a color, a backdrop and a piece of music), with a switch that stills its backdrop.
- **Bank name**: the bank's initials in any of the 24 official languages of the EU (ECB, EZB, BCE, EKT and the others). The title follows the choice.
- **Game**: the settings for every game. New games has the five switches of what a new game hides at first (the historical choice and the "In history" box, the expected Council vote, the Troika label, the Draghi measure label, the advisors' forecasts); the form for a new game shows them under Difficulty, as Hidden in this game. With a game open, This game has a button to Game settings. During a game has autosave on or off and whether a click beside the year report closes it (off by default). Achievements shows how many are earned, with Clear achievements, which asks first. Help has a button that shows the guided tour again.
- **Game settings** (a toolbar button and the key G, while a game is open): the settings of the open game, apart from the settings for every game. A warning at the top says that large changes during a game can unbalance it. Difficulty sets the level (Normal, Realistic, Hard or Custom, with the four values of Custom); Area of responsibility sets the items a year; Hidden in this game has its five switches. Each change applies at once and is saved with the game; a change of level is noted in the archive and kept in `G.levelLog`.
- **Difficulty**: chosen with each new game and kept with the run. Normal is the game as it was.

  Realistic makes Council members vote yes less readily, gives more weight to the gap between a member's stance and an option and to the member's relation with the president, and makes pressuring governments, speeches and the yearly rise from crisis pressure and trust raise a proposal's support by 70% of the Normal amount, so a policy needs members on its side before it passes.

  Hard goes further: members vote yes least readily, the relation decides close votes, and support rises by half the Normal amount. Custom sets each of the four values with a slider, from easier than Normal to harder than Hard.

  EU enlargement, under the difficulty, lists eleven states outside the EU, each with the year the run assumes it joins: Montenegro, Albania, Moldova, Ukraine, Serbia, North Macedonia, Scotland, Iceland, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Georgia and Kosovo. None is chosen by default. A chosen state brings required items: its accession in its year, then its application for the euro; for Montenegro and Kosovo, which use the euro without an agreement, an item two years before; for Scotland, the referendum of 2028 and its currency at independence in 2030. Until then the map draws the United Kingdom whole. The form marks Scotland as outside Selk's history, where it stays in the United Kingdom. In a game from 2041, a state due to join by 2041 starts as a member. [Data and sources](../DATA.md) gives the years and their sources.
- **Area of responsibility**: chosen with each new game beside the difficulty, kept with the run, and changeable in Game settings, from the next year. It sets how many optional decisions a year brings. Narrow fills a calm year to 2 items and a crisis year to 3, Standard to 3 and 4, Broad to 4 and 5. The rate decision, the year's events, requests for a loan program and the essential items come at every level, so a year can hold more than the number set, up to 6. The essential items are emergency liquidity, savers leaving a state's banks, a bank that needs capital, a state that loses its investment grade, and a loss in the bank's accounts.
- **Keyboard** (shown only with a keyboard connected: a screen with a mouse or trackpad counts as having one, and a touch screen as having one once a key is pressed outside a text field): Shortcuts holds the switch for all keyboard shortcuts. Where they work holds a switch for each area: the agenda, the end of the year, views, the toolbar, dialogs, the ending film and time-lapse, the end screens, the title screens and the form for a new game, and debug; they are disabled while the shortcuts are off. Hints sets how far the key hints show, for the areas switched on: beside every button (the default), beside the main button of each screen with every other key in its button's tooltip, in tooltips only, or off; the keys for the options of the agenda show with the first two. A toolbar button always shows its key in its tooltip. The choice is kept in `kbdHints` (`S.kbdLevel` in `js/core/dom.js`). Key bindings lists every action with its key, area by area, as a game's controls do: a click on a key waits for the next key pressed (Esc cancels), a key that another action of the same context holds swaps with it, so every action keeps a key, and each action has a button back to its default, with Reset all keys below. 1 to 9 (the options) and Tab are fixed. The keys changed are kept in `keyBind`. Tab, and Enter or Space on a focused button, always work. See `js/ui/keys.js`.
- **Sound**: General holds the switch for all sound, which the toolbar button also sets, and presets for the kind of speaker (laptop, headphones, phone, desktop or TV). Playlist picks the European soundtrack or its resistance variants. Turning the soundtrack off stops the music at once, and leaving the Sound page or closing Settings ends a preview. Interface sounds holds their switch, a master volume, and a level for clicks, alerts, votes, the end of a year, saves, and typing and the pointer, each slider with a button back to its default. Sound set holds the seed that reshapes every sound at once, with icon buttons at the right end of its field, as in KDE's spin box: - and +, which step the seed by one (from 0 to 999 999), then Random and The set as designed. Groups of sounds lists the groups; a group opens on a page of its own, at its top, and going back returns to the place in the Sound page where it was opened, with the focus on that group; with a head as in the notes (a button back, then the group's name), that gives every sound its check box, volume with a reset, pitch and Play, as the soundtrack's pieces have; typing and the pointer are off at first. Music holds the soundtrack, the playlist, the music volume, In the background, which keeps the music playing while the tab is hidden, and each piece. Controls that cannot apply while sound or the soundtrack is off are disabled, with the reason.

  A soundtrack, off by default, follows the state of the game with nine pieces: Eurocrisis by default, Draghi for three years after a Draghi measure, Troika while the bank sits in a Troika, Superbad when a Troika and a Draghi measure come together, Bankrupt when a run ends badly, Ode to Joy (the European anthem) when a period ends no better than the record, Finally when a period ends well or in calm years, October for the move into the Federation, and Kremlin for the federal years.

  Before a game, Strasbourg plays, or the title theme's own piece. The music has its own volume, each piece can be turned off or set louder or softer, and it always stays below the interface sounds.
- **Motion and text**: follow the system, reduce or full motion; even line breaks (on by default) for paragraphs, headings, labels, pills, table cells, menus, tooltips and popovers, the agenda and glossary, and the rest of the window, each with its own switch, with a small script for browsers that lack `text-wrap: pretty`; in a group of their own, Glossary and footnotes: links from terms in the text to the glossary and the numbered footnotes on the cards, each on by default with its own switch; normal or large text; the date format of saves and achievements, in any of the numeric formats used in Europe (DD-MM-YYYY by default, the international format understood across the EU, DD.MM.YYYY, DD/MM/YYYY, D. M. YYYY, D. M. YYYY., D.M.YYYY, YYYY-MM-DD and YYYY. MM. DD.), with times in 24 hours.

  Under reduced motion the turn transition, the Council vote, moving charts and values, and the ending film still play, because they show information; the film keeps its order and timing, and its changes become plain fades.
- **Storage**, in three groups:
  - Where the game is kept: in this tab only (the default) or on this computer.
  - Kept data: a list in the style of the groups of sounds, each item with its name, where it is kept, its size and its key, and View and Delete as icon buttons; View opens the contents under the item.
  - File operations, last: Game files exports the game in progress with its saves, exports all games, or imports a file of saves (checked, then added as an import of its own after a confirmation; [Saved state](saved-state.md) has the details). Delete data deletes the game data and keeps the settings, or deletes everything, including the settings: the debug settings, Wipe data on refresh and the save location, which returns to This tab. Reset all settings returns every setting to its default, including the debug settings and Wipe data on refresh, and keeps the saves, the achievements and the save location.
- **Debug**: the Debug mode switch, which grants every tool below it and disables them all while it is off; a panel with tools for testing, a log in the browser console, a fast mode, a switch that shows the debug bar above the agenda (off by default), a switch that marks the options on the intended path, a switch that announces the achievements a run earns by the rules without keeping them (Achievement notices, off by default; each is announced once until the page is reloaded), a switch that asks before a jump reaches the end of the period (Jumps into an ending, on by default), and a button that skips to the ending film of any ending. Testing holds Wipe data on refresh, which erases the game at every reload and works with debug mode off, and Keep debug through wipes, which keeps the debug settings, Wipe data on refresh and the debug panel's position through every wipe (Wipe data on refresh, Delete all game data and Reset all settings); with This computer chosen, both Debug and Storage say that the two disagree. The panel's close button only hides it; the Debug tag in the title bar shows it again. See [Running and debugging](running.md).
